How the damage and casualty numbers are worked out
  • Blast loads: Kingery-Bulmash (IATG 01.80 §5.1.2). The engine reproduces IATG Table 5 (e.g. 1000 kg at 50 m: 0.43 bar, 5.9 bar·ms, 1.01 bar reflected, 37.9 ms, 82.4 ms, 398 m/s). Dynamic pressure q = 5/2·P²/(7P₀+P) (IATG Table 6).
  • Brick housing (IATG §6.2.1, Tables 40–42): Rx = Kx·M1/3 / (1+(3175/M)²)1/6, using the Gilbert, Lees & Scilly K-factors A 4.8, B 7.1, Cb 12.4, Ca 21.3, D 42.6. M is the TNT-equivalent mass.
  • Windows (IATG §6.2.3, Tables 43–46): Swiss P-I model (P−A)(I−B)=C with the small/medium/large probits. The model uses incident pressure and impulse. Occupant injury from breakage follows Table 46 (100% breakage → 100% minor, 10% major, 1% lethal).
  • Other building types: lognormal fragility curves (Light / Moderate / Severe / Destroyed) with medians taken from the commonly cited damage-vs-overpressure ranges and your spreadsheet. Dispersion β = 0.35. That range table is widely attributed to FEMA 426 (2003), but it does not appear in FEMA 426 / BIPS-06 (2011). The protection-level wording follows FEMA 426 / BIPS-06 Table 3-2 (UFC 4-010-01).
  • People inside: casualty probabilities by damage category from IATG Table 54 (Gilbert, Lees & Scilly). For FEMA building types the states map to A / B / Cb / Ca. These combine with primary blast and window-glass injury, treated as independent.
  • IATG model (default): IATG Table 50 / TNO Green Book lung lethality. Pr = 5 + 5.7 ln V, with V = (P/P₀)/(4.17 − 0.00164·ln t/t + 0.0161/t), where t is the positive-phase duration (s) for a 70 kg body. It reproduces the 11% lethality in IATG Table 53 when reflected pressure is used. Standing in the open uses side-on + dynamic pressure. Next to a wall uses reflected pressure. Eardrum uses the TNO probit −12.6 + 1.524 ln P.
  • Spreadsheet model: Eisenberg probits. Lethality: −77.1 + 6.91 ln P; eardrum: −15.6 + 1.93 ln P. They reproduce your sheet’s 14.5–29 psi and 2.4–12.2 psi rows. They are based on long-duration (nuclear-scale) blasts, so they overstate deaths from small charges.
  • UFC 3-340-02 model: lognormal curves through Table 3-1 (eardrum 5/15, lung 30/80, lethality 100/130/200 psi). These ignore duration.
  • Being thrown (TNO): head impact Y = 5 − 8.49 ln(2430/P + 4·10⁸/(P·i)); whole-body displacement Y = 5 − 2.44 ln(7380/P + 1.3·10⁹/(P·i)). P is in Pa and i in Pa·s.
  • In the open: lacerations use a lognormal curves fitted to your sheet: light injuries from about 1 psi (median 1.5 psi), serious lacerations median 3 psi (sheet: 2 psi injured, 3 psi serious common, 5 psi universal). Killed = the highest of primary lethality, head impact and body displacement.
  • Crowd estimate: integrates the per-person probabilities over the ground area at the given density. Everyone inside the fireball limit is counted as killed.
  • Not modelled: fragments and debris (IATG §6.3.3 needs case- and debris-density data), shielding, and channelling between buildings. Real casualties from a cased device or vehicle bomb are often driven by fragments. Treat these numbers as planning estimates with large uncertainty.

Definitions

Shock wave: a pressure pulse travelling faster than sound.
Positive phase: the first outward pressure pulse. Pressure jumps almost instantly to its peak, then decays exponentially.
Negative phase: the underpressure (partial vacuum) that follows the shock front.
Incident (side-on) pressure: the shock overpressure in open, unobstructed air. This is the main value in the tables and chart.
Reflected pressure: the overpressure on a surface facing the blast. The shock stops against the surface, so the pressure is higher (2× to 8× or more).
Dynamic pressure: the drag force of the air flowing behind the shock wave.
Scaled distance Z = R / W1/3 (m/kg1/3), from Hopkinson-Cranz cube-root scaling.

Method, validity and limitations
  • The model uses the Kingery & Bulmash (1984, ARBL-TR-02555) polynomial fits for a hemispherical TNT surface burst: log10Y = Σ CiUi, with U = C0 + C1·log10Z. This is the method in IATG 01.80 §6.2 and the UN SaferGuard KB calculator. The coefficients were cross-checked against Swisdak’s (1994) simplified fits and agree within about 1%.
  • The KB fits are valid for 0.0674 ≤ Z ≤ 40 m/kg1/3. Outside that range the tool shows italic values. Incident pressure and impulse use Swisdak’s long-range fits (up to Z ≈ 198 and 159). Reflected pressure uses the ideal-gas normal-reflection relation. Arrival time and duration are left blank.
  • TNT equivalence: the pressure factor scales pressure, arrival time, duration and shock velocity. The impulse factor scales impulse.
  • The free-air option treats the charge as a surface burst of W/1.8 (assumes ground reflection factor 1.8). It is a first approximation only and ignores Mach-stem formation for low air bursts.
  • The model does not include fragmentation, casing effects, terrain, confinement, internal or channelled blasts, or reflections from nearby structures. Fragments often reach much further than the blast hazard distances.
  • Shock front velocity uses the Rankine-Hugoniot relation U = a0√(1 + 6Ps/7P0), with a0 = 340.3 m/s and P0 = 101.325 kPa.
  • This tool is for planning and training. It does not replace qualified assessment.
