How to read this page

This page loads the live calculator (index.html) in the background and calls the same functions the calculator uses. It compares their output with values published in the standards and with your original spreadsheet. If anyone changes a formula, a coefficient or a TNT factor, the matching check fails here straight away.

PASS within the stated tolerance (tolerances allow for rounding in the published tables). FAIL outside the tolerance. INFO a known, documented difference, shown for transparency and not counted as a failure.

Sources and how each one is used

“Verification” describes how this app’s implementation was checked against the source. It does not rate the source itself.

Known limitations and honest caveats

  • The Kingery-Bulmash coefficients are the published hemispherical-surface-burst set (ARBRL-TR-02555, as reproduced in AASTP-1 Table 5-5). AASTP-1 itself was not available here. The coefficients are proven by matching IATG Table 5 and by agreeing with Swisdak’s independent 1994 fits (both checked live below).
  • Beyond Z = 40 m/kg1/3 (outside KB validity) the app extends incident pressure and impulse using Swisdak’s long-range slopes, joined continuously at Z = 40. IATG notes that Kinney-Graham is validated to Z = 500. Results in this range are shown in italics in the app.
  • The damage vs overpressure ranges (glass 0.15–0.22 psi … total destruction 10–12 psi) are widely attributed to FEMA 426 (2003). A review of FEMA 426 / BIPS-06 (2011) found they are not in that edition, and no standard in the reference set contains them. They are shown as indicative. The building fragility medians and the β = 0.35 dispersion built on them are engineering judgement, not a FEMA-published fragility model.
  • HME TNT-equivalence factors (TATP, HMTD, improvised ANFO, AN/Al, urea nitrate, chlorate mixtures, neat AN) are indicative only. No standard in the reference set gives them. They come from open literature, and real performance varies widely.
  • Injury models disagree by design. The Eisenberg probits behind the spreadsheet come from long-duration blasts and overstate lethality from small charges. UFC 3-340-02 Table 3-1 ignores duration. IATG Table 50 (TNO) includes duration. The app shows all three side by side.
  • Fragments and debris from casings or vehicles are not modelled. IATG §6.3.3 needs case- and debris-density data that a quick assessment does not have.
  • The spreadsheet’s 110 m / 15 kg entry (3.06 kPa) is inconsistent with the rest of its column. KB gives 2.06 kPa, so it looks like a transcription error. It is listed as INFO, not compared.
